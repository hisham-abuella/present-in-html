The idea

A slide file plays back what you made. A web page runs.

So a slide can hold a working app, a simulation, sound, code, a game or another page. Press the right arrow.

Everything that follows is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript inside this one file. No plug-ins, no add-ins, nothing to install.

Web app

Ask “what if?” and get the answer live

Free-space path loss: how much a radio signal weakens on its way. Move the sliders, or pick a band.

FSPL = 20 log(d) + 20 log(f) − 147.55

103.3dB lost

This is a calculator, not a picture of one. The formula is the standard free-space path loss with distance in metres and frequency in hertz. The solid line is the loss over distance at the chosen frequency; the dashed line is 2.4 GHz for comparison. Jump from Wi-Fi to mmWave and you see why 28 gigahertz cells are small. A spreadsheet chart on a slide can't take a question from the room; this can.

Simulation

Physics you can drag around

Two antennas send the same wave. Where the waves meet in step they add up; out of step they cancel.

Drag an antenna. Change the phase and the beam turns: that is beamforming.

Each pixel adds the two waves at its distance from each antenna, every frame, in plain JavaScript. Bright is where they reinforce, dark is where they cancel. Shift the phase of the second antenna and the bright lobes swing to one side: that is the idea behind beamforming in 5G, shown live instead of described. Antennas can be moved with a finger, a mouse, or the arrow keys once they have focus.

Sound and voice

The slide can sing, and talk

The tone is made by the browser as you listen, and drawn on the scope. The voice is the browser's own.

Turn your sound on first.

Close to A4, the note orchestras tune to.

Two browser features, no files. Web Audio builds the tone from an oscillator while you listen, and an analyser draws the real waveform on the canvas. The voice is speech synthesis, which every modern browser ships. It reads this slide's title and text, so a deck could narrate itself, or read slides aloud for someone who can't see them.

Video

Quick edits, right in the browser

A recording of the deck editor: retype a word, swap Q3 for Q4 across the deck, add a note, save the same file.

It plays while this slide is on screen and stops when you leave. The buttons jump inside it.

The video is a screen recording of editor.html, the small text editor that ships with the skill. It opens a deck, lets you click any text on a slide and type, find and replace across every slide, edit speaker notes, and saves the same file, touching only the words you changed. The slide plays the clip when you arrive and pauses it when you leave, and the chapter buttons seek inside it, which is a few lines of JavaScript.
