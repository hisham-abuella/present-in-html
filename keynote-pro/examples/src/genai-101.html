Step two

Every word becomes a point in space

Step two. Each word becomes a point in space, and closeness is measured by the angle between the arrows, not by the letters two words share. Cat and kitten sit close; cat and truck do not. Warn that real embeddings have hundreds of dimensions squeezed into two here, so close in the picture is not exactly similar to the model. Bridge: now the word it.

Step three

Attention decides which earlier words matter

Step three. To fill a blank after it, the model has to know what it refers to. Attention is how it looks back: the thick line goes to cat, the thin ones to everything else. Imagine memorising every LEGO combination: impossible, the table explodes. Counting words only works on sentences you have already seen. Embeddings and attention are the way out of that explosion. Walk down the three rows and let the highlighted column carry the argument. Bridge: so how does a model actually get built?

How a model gets built

Read, then practise, then take feedback

  1. Pre-training

    Guess the next word in real text, over and over. The text is its own answer key.

  2. Fine-tuning

    Learn from worked examples of good answers to real requests.

  3. Alignment

    People compare two answers and pick the better one.

Three stages, one click each. Only stage one needs a supercomputer. Stages two and three are comparatively cheap, which is why many assistants sit on a handful of base models. Pre-training is the text as its own answer key; fine-tuning shows good answers; alignment is people picking the better one. Bridge: back to the output, and why answers vary.

Sampling

Why the same prompt gives different words

    import random
    probs = {"mat": .42, "floor": .23, "sofa": .16,
             "bed": .11, "roof": .07, "moon": .01}

    def next_word(probs):
        words, weights = zip(*probs.items())
        return random.choices(words, weights)[0]  # a draw, not the top pick

The model samples from the distribution rather than always taking the top word. Step through the code with the focus builds: the data, the function, then the whole thing. Run it twice live if you can: two different words, and neither is a mistake. Bridge: one line to take away on the next slide.
