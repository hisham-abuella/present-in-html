The site and the machine

Eighty turbines, 45 km out, in 38 m of water.

Section layout with a photo behind it. The deck's own style block adds the photo and a dark wash, and switches the text to the theme's on-image colour.

One turn of the rotor powers a home for three days.

Statement layout: one sentence, set big, nothing else. The accent word is in an em. A 15 MW turbine at full output turns about once every eight seconds and makes over 30 kWh in that time, roughly three days for an average European home.

Why this stretch of sea

  • Steady wind. 10.2 m/s average at hub height.
  • Shallow banks. 30 to 45 m, deep enough for monopiles.
  • A grid with room. Spare capacity at the coastal substation.
  • A port close by. 70 km to a quay that can take the blades.

Bullets layout with data-build="list": each point arrives on a click. Bold lead, then one short line of support.

Monopile or jacket

Monopile

Jacket

A four-legged lattice pinned with small piles. Lighter in steel, slower to install, and the choice for the deep north-east corner.

Power in the wind

One turbine at 10 m/s

P = ½ρAv3Cp = 12.9 MW
ρ
Air density, 1.225 kg/m³
A
Swept area, 43,700 m²
Cp
Power coefficient, 0.48

Formula layout: the equation, the result highlighted, and the terms below. Half of 1.225 times 43,700 times 1,000, times 0.48, is about 12.9 megawatts. Power grows with the cube of wind speed, which is why the site matters so much.

What happened

Vibration rose over six hours in a storm. The turbine stopped itself before anyone was called.

Why we chose a bigger turbine

  1. The port can handle blades up to 120 m long.

  2. Every foundation costs the same, whatever sits on top.

  3. Fewer, larger turbines

    Eighty instead of a hundred: twenty fewer foundations and cables.

Rows layout: labelled rows, here situation, complication, resolution. data-hot highlights the one that matters.
