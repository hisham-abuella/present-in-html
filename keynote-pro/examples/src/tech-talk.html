p99 under 200 ms

What we learned replacing a polling job queue with partitioned leases.

Workers polled a Postgres table

The fix

2

Lease, don’t scan

Partition the work, then claim it without blocking.

Section break: the fix. One sentence: partition the work, then claim it without blocking. Give the room a moment to reset before the details. If you are short on time, this is the slide where you check the clock; the next twelve minutes are the core of the talk. Bridge: the new shape.

After

Partitioned lanes, one lease each

Same boxes, new shape. Four lanes means four workers never touch each other's rows. Each worker owns a lane, and claims skip rows another worker holds. Click once per lane. Point at what disappeared: the shared table everyone fought over. Bridge: follow a single job through the system.

Life of a job

Four steps, no polling

  1. Enqueue

    The API writes the job to its lane, chosen by hashing the tenant.

  2. Lease

    A worker claims a batch for 30 seconds.

  3. Execute

    Work runs; the lease is renewed while it does.

  4. Ack

    The row is deleted. A missed ack simply expires.

Four steps, no polling: enqueue, lease, execute, ack. Emphasise the expiry: crash recovery is just a lease running out, no special code path. That is the idea to carry out of the room. The job is hashed to its lane by tenant. Bridge: the lease is one query. Show it.

Lease

The whole trick is one query

    -- claim up to 10 ready jobs without waiting on anyone
    UPDATE jobs SET state = 'leased',
      lease_until = now() + interval '30 seconds'
    WHERE id IN (
      SELECT id FROM jobs WHERE state = 'ready' AND lane = 2
      ORDER BY run_at LIMIT 10 FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED)
    RETURNING *;

Walk the focus: the comment, the update, the subquery, then the whole thing. SKIP LOCKED is the line that removes the waiting; say so plainly. Mention that the lease length is thirty seconds here and renewed while work runs. Keep the pace slow, this is the slide people photograph. Bridge: did it work?

Sample data, eight weeks of rollout. The cliff in week three is when the first tenants moved to lanes. The long tail after is stragglers still on the old path. End on week eight: 180 milliseconds, under the 200 target in the title. Be honest that the data is illustrative. Bridge: why not something simpler?

Three options. Keep polling was the status quo; the Redis list was faster to build but lost jobs on failover; leases reused Postgres, so no new on-call surface. Read down the last row: a dead worker means a stuck job, a lost job, or a lease that expires. Bridge: what would we do differently?

Looking back

What worked

  • Hashing by tenant kept lanes even
  • Shadow-writing to both queues for two weeks
  • One dashboard: lease age, not queue depth

What we’d change

  • Start with 16 lanes, not 4
  • Alert on lease expiry rate from day one
  • Write the rollback runbook before the migration

Be honest here: four lanes was too few and we resharded once. Left side, what worked: hashing by tenant, shadow-writing for two weeks, one dashboard about lease age. Right side, what we would change. Invite the room to disagree on the alerting point. Bridge: the result after ninety days.
