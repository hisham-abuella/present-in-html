Theorem

The Nyquist condition

fs > 2 fmax
fs
Sampling frequency, samples per second
fmax
Highest frequency present in the signal
2fmax
The Nyquist rate, the minimum to avoid aliasing
Example
Audio to 20 kHz needs more than 40 kHz; CDs use 44.1 kHz

Strict inequality: sampling exactly at 2 f_max can fail for a sine at its peak crossings.

Demo

See aliasing in five lines

    import numpy as np
    t = np.arange(0, 1, 1/100)          # fs = 100 Hz
    x = np.sin(2*np.pi*90*t)            # a 90 Hz tone
    # 90 Hz is above fs/2, so it appears at 10 Hz
    plt.plot(t, x)

Run it live. The 90 Hz tone shows up as a (inverted) 10 Hz wave: 100 minus 90.

Check

A 6 kHz tone is sampled at 8 kHz. What frequency appears?

  1. 6 kHz
  2. 4 kHz
  3. 2 kHz
  4. 14 kHz

8 minus 6 equals 2 kHz. Ask them to explain why 4 kHz is tempting (it is fs/2).

Take away

  • Sample above twice the highest frequency.
  • Filter first. An anti-aliasing filter removes what you cannot capture.
  • Aliases cannot be undone after sampling.
