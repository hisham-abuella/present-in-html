The river and the city

How a fictional port town grew around its ferry, 1780 to 1890, told through the objects that survived.

Harbour Museum, a fictional institutionCurator's talkOctober 2026

Entirely fictional town and collection. Orient the audience: they will meet five objects in the next gallery.

The ferry bell rang at six, and the whole town set its day by it.
Harbour Gazette, 1824Fictional newspaper, sample text

Read the line slowly. It is the thesis in miniature: the river set the town's clock.

Crossings per year

The ferry peaked just before the bridge ended it.

The 1857 point is a partial year, ending when the bridge opened in the spring. Say so, or someone will ask.

Interpretation

Two readings of the same decline

The economic story

  • A faster, free crossing replaced a paid one
  • Trade followed the bridge
  • Quayside rents fell by half

The social story

  • The bell lost its role as the town clock
  • Ferry families moved into other trades
  • Memory outlasted the work

Both are supported by the sources. Let the audience see that history is argued, not just recorded.
