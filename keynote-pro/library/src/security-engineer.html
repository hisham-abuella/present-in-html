Fictional incident at a fictional company, Harbor Freight Cloud. No real systems, IPs or customers. Blameless format: talk about controls, not people.

Impact

412 accounts

Logged in from attacker infrastructure. 37 had saved payment methods; none were charged.

Source: auth logs, reviewed by two engineers

Lead with the impact so nobody waits for it. 412 successful logins out of 1.9 million attempts.

Note the 6.5 hour gap between first request and alert. The detection threshold was the weak spot, not the response.

Failed logins per minute, peak window

Low and slow: the attack stayed under our per-IP limit all night.

The curve is what the attacker wanted: slow ramp, no single IP above 3 attempts a minute. Per-IP limits could not see it.

Root cause

Threat, gap, consequence

  1. Reused passwords from unrelated breaches, tried at scale.

  2. Limits were per IP, no check against known-breached passwords, MFA optional.

  3. 412 sessions, 37 with stored payment methods, no card data exposed.

  4. Detect on the target, not the source

    Rate and anomaly rules keyed on accounts and on the failure ratio.

Keep it blameless. The gap is in the controls.

The 15 minute target is tested by replaying last night's traffic against the new rule in staging.

Response plan

Four steps, in this order

  1. Contain

    Force resets on the 412 accounts.

  2. Detect

    Ship account-level anomaly alert.

  3. Prevent

    Breached-password check and MFA.

  4. Verify

    Replay the attack monthly.

Owners and dates are in the tracker. Containment is done; detection ships this sprint.
