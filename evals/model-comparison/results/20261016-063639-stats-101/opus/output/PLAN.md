# STATS 101, Module 1: Data, samples and describing data

- **Audience**: first-year undergraduates, no statistics background (high-school algebra only)
- **Occasion and time**: lecture, 50 minutes, the quiz runs inside the slot
- **Goal**: students can ask three questions of any data set and answer them with a graph and a few numbers
- **Promise**: By the end you can tell where data come from, what kind they are, and what they say (slide 1, repeated on 2)
- **Story**: How data are collected matters more than how much there is, and a picture plus a few resistant numbers tells you what they say.
- **Fence**: a bigger sample does not fix bias; correlation is not causation; the mean is not always the right "average"
- **Look**: `chalk` (library theme for university lecturers, Literata / Karla), lang en; a slate board suits a maths lecture
- **Sources**: stats-101-module-1.md (sections 1 to 18)
- **Placeholders**: two images (slide 3 collage of everyday statistics; slide 4 John Snow's 1854 map)
