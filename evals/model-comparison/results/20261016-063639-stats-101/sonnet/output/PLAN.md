# STATS 101, Module 1: Data, Samples and Describing Data

- **Audience**: first-year undergraduates, no statistics background, high-school algebra
- **Occasion and time**: one lecture, 50 minutes
- **Promise**: By the end you can tell a sample from a population, pick a graph, compute centre and spread, and explain why correlation is not causation.
- **Story**: How data are collected matters most; plot first, then summarise with centre and spread, and never read causation into correlation.
- **Fence**: descriptive statistics summarise the data we have; inference (later modules) is not today.
- **Look**: studio theme, theme fonts, English; teaching look with amber/cream diagrams.
- **Sources**: stats-101-module-1.md
- **Placeholders**: two image placeholders (Snow's map, Literary Digest ballot)

29 slides in order: title, objectives, definition, branches, Snow, Literary Digest numbers, takeaway, pop/sample, variables, levels, observational vs experiment, experiment design, sampling, bias, bias fence, graphs, Anscombe, shape, centre, outlier effect, spread, SD formula, box plot, z, 68-95-99.7, correlation, quiz x2, summary.
